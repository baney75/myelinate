Section A

1
B. P50 is the PO₂ that half-saturates Hb. Needing more PO₂ to get there means lower affinity (right shift). In the Hill model, saturation at 40 mmHg falls from about 74% to 63%, so more O₂ is released.
  • A (P50 read backwards): a higher number feels like "more binding." It means the opposite.
  • C (affinity confused with capacity): capacity depends on Hb amount, which did not change.
  • D (plateau ignored): at 100 mmHg the curve is flat; S drops only from about 97% to 95%.
O1 · S1, S2
2
D. Cooperativity: binding shifts the tetramer from the T (tense) toward the R (relaxed) state, raising the affinity of the remaining sites. The Hill coefficient of about 2.7 (greater than 1) measures this.
  • A (independent sites): sites filling from highest to lowest affinity would flatten the curve, not make it sigmoid.
  • B (plasma limits binding): dissolved O₂ rises linearly with PO₂ (0.003 mL/dL per mmHg); plasma does not saturate.
  • C (2,3-BPG role reversed): 2,3-BPG binds deoxy-Hb and lowers affinity.
O1 · S2
3
C. Working muscle makes CO₂, acid and heat. All three lower affinity and shift the curve right, so more O₂ unloads where it is needed.
  • A (CO₂ thought to be consumed): muscle produces CO₂; falling PCO₂ shifts left.
  • B (pH scale read backwards): more H⁺ means lower pH; and cooling shifts left.
  • D (temperature effect reversed): heat lowers affinity; cooling raises it.
O2 · S2
4
A. Gases diffuse down their partial-pressure gradient. Only dissolved O₂ diffuses, and its amount is set by PO₂. Hb-bound O₂ adds to content but does not drive diffusion.
  • B (content gradient): the stem's own case refutes it: fetal content can be higher while O₂ still flows in.
  • C (saturation as driving force): saturation reflects affinity as well as PO₂.
  • D (Hb pulls O₂): Hb raises carrying capacity; it does not set direction.
O5 · S2
5
B. HbF's γ chains bind 2,3-BPG weakly, so HbF escapes that right shift (P50 about 19 vs 26.6 mmHg). In the model at 30 mmHg: HbF about 77%, HbA about 58%. High-affinity HbF keeps fetal plasma PO₂ low, so the maternal-to-fetal gradient persists.
  • A (fetal Hb has lower affinity): reasoning from "the fetus must release O₂" gets the direction backwards.
  • C (only Hb amount matters): fetal Hb concentration is higher, but affinity also differs.
  • D (2,3-BPG effect reversed): stronger 2,3-BPG binding would lower affinity.
O3 · S2, S4
6
A. Within minutes, hyperventilation causes respiratory alkalosis, so lower PCO₂ and higher pH shift the curve left. The 2,3-BPG rise (a right shift) builds over hours to days.
  • B (time course mixed up): true for days later, not the first 30 minutes.
  • C (PO₂ treated as a shifter): a lower PO₂ moves the point down the curve; it does not shift the curve.
  • D (blood chemistry thought not to touch hemoglobin): blowing off CO₂ lowers H⁺ (CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻), and less CO₂ and H⁺ bound to Hb raises its O₂ affinity (Bohr effect in reverse).
O2 · transfer · S2
7
C. CO binds heme with roughly 200–250 times O₂'s affinity, cutting O₂ content. PaO₂ measures dissolved O₂, so it stays normal. CO also raises the O₂ affinity of the remaining sites, a left shift that impairs unloading.
  • A (PaO₂ confused with bound O₂): CO competes for heme, not for dissolved O₂.
  • B (CO shifts right): the shift is left, which makes things worse.
  • D (PaO₂ = content): content depends on functional Hb and saturation.
O3 · S3
8
D. 2,3-BPG lives inside each red cell. Without it, transfused cells are left-shifted and hold their O₂ until they rebuild 2,3-BPG over the following days.
  • A (direction of the 2,3-BPG effect reversed): 2,3-BPG lowers affinity, so losing it raises affinity.
  • B (only the environment sets the curve): intracellular 2,3-BPG travels with the cell.
  • C (content confused with affinity): the cells' Hb per cell is not why they release less.

Section B

9
Model answer. The Bohr effect. H⁺ binds hemoglobin (protonating histidine residues) and CO₂ binds its amino ends as carbamino groups; both stabilize the deoxy (T) state and lower O₂ affinity. The curve shifts right, so at the same tissue PO₂ less O₂ stays bound and more is unloaded where metabolism is highest.
  1. 1 pt: names the Bohr effect, or says right shift / lower affinity.
  2. 1 pt: mechanism: H⁺ and/or CO₂ bind Hb and favor the deoxy (T) state.
  3. 1 pt: consequence: lower saturation at the same tissue PO₂, so more O₂ unloaded in that muscle.
O2 · S2 (Bohr, Hasselbalch & Krogh 1904)
10
Model answer. Both have about 10.5 g/dL of Hb able to carry O₂ (15 × 0.70), so content is similar. But in Y, CO raises the O₂ affinity of the remaining sites (left shift). Y's Hb gives up less O₂ at a given tissue PO₂, so tissue PO₂ must fall lower before the O₂ is released.
  1. 1 pt: functional Hb, and so content, is similar.
  2. 1 pt: CO left-shifts the curve of the remaining Hb (raises affinity).
  3. 1 pt: so less O₂ unloads at tissue PO₂ (or tissue PO₂ must fall lower). Do not require CO's effect on cytochrome oxidase; it is a true extra.
