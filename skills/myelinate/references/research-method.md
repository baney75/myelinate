# Research that changes the lesson

Use this for substantial topics, uncertain claims, conflicting materials, or unavailable sources. Scale effort to the question. There is no minimum citation count that makes research deep.

## A bounded investigation

Turn the learning goal into answerable questions: what is the mechanism, what assumptions does it need, what commonly goes wrong, how is it used, and what changed recently? Search each necessary question, read the strongest sources, and follow their evidence trail where it matters. A capable parallel research worker can investigate an independent question when available; the tutor must reconcile its sources and remains responsible for the lesson.

For stable foundations, established textbooks and disciplinary reference works can be the best teaching sources. For a contested empirical claim, compare the primary studies and a reputable synthesis; inspect design, population, outcomes, uncertainty, and applicability. For technology, bind advice to the actual version. For fast-changing or consequential practice, verify the relevant current authority and date. Separate teaching about a field from personalized medical, legal, or financial advice.

Search for a claim's strongest reasonable counterevidence, especially when the first source advertises a product or a single dramatic result. Syndicated copies are not independent corroboration. Do not claim consensus by counting search results. If access is only an abstract, attribute only what that abstract supports. Seek an authorized open version or use another source; never imply full-text review.

## Source ledger

Keep only what is useful. For each substantive source:

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Title, author/organization, year, edition/version, DOI or direct URL. |
| Inspection | Retrieval date and exact section/page actually read; full text, abstract only, or supplied excerpt. |
| Role | What claim, definition, worked method, or course objective it supports. |
| Limits | Population/context, disagreement, uncertainty, inaccessible portion, or date sensitivity. |

In the learner's answer, keep citations close to the claims and explanations readable. Separate “the source reports,” “my synthesis,” and “this is a proposed practice schedule.” Do not cite a general home page for a specific claim it does not support.

## Course conflicts and missing material

- Extract tested scope from the actual course evidence. If it is not supplied, call the lesson provisional and identify the needed document.
- Different editions can change notation, chapter numbering, and assigned scope. Verify the edition before giving chapter/page directions.
- An instructor's notation is usually worth preserving. A factual contradiction deserves a respectful explanation of the discrepancy and a source; do not silently teach it as true or pretend to know what the instructor will grade.
- Do not reproduce a copyrighted textbook chapter or publish private slides. Create original explanations and practice; quote only what is needed and permitted. Keep exam answers and restricted material within the course's stated rules.

## When research cannot finish

State the specific missing source, access failure, or unresolved conflict. Teach what can be supported and narrow the conclusion. For unavailable browsing, label the stable explanation as not freshly verified and work from supplied content. A useful lesson can be provisional; a fabricated source cannot.
