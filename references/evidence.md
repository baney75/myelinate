# What the method rests on

Source review: **2026-10-02**. These sources support components of the teaching method. They do not validate Myelinate as a complete intervention. Stable foundational findings belong beside newer work; “newest” is not a quality score. Re-check this record when updating a claim.

The notes below are deliberately bounded to the inspected publisher abstracts, institutional records, or full-text/guideline pages indicated. Do not imply that an abstract-only source was reviewed in full.

| Practice | Source and inspected scope | What it supports—and what it does not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieve, then return later | Roediger & Karpicke (2006), *Test-Enhanced Learning: Taking Memory Tests Improves Long-Term Retention*. [Publisher abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x). | University prose-learning experiments favored testing over restudy at two days/one week, while restudy did better at five minutes. This does not prove every quiz helps every subject. |
| Space practice | Cepeda et al. (2006), *Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks: A Review and Quantitative Synthesis*. [Institutional abstract/record](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1771/). | Synthesis of 317 experiments in 184 articles; useful spacing depends on the final retention interval. It does not supply a universal 1–3–7-day rule. |
| Correct errors after retrieval | Butler & Roediger (2008), *Feedback enhances the positive effects and reduces the negative effects of multiple-choice testing*. [Publisher abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/MC.36.3.604). | Correct-answer feedback increased later correct recall and reduced intrusion of misleading choices in passage-based experiments. This does not establish an added benefit of explanatory feedback or one optimal feedback time. |
| Be flexible about feedback timing | Ryan et al. (2024), *Timing's not everything: Immediate and delayed feedback are equally beneficial for performance in formative multiple-choice testing*. [Publisher full text](https://asmepublications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/medu.15287). | In 41 second-year medical students, item versus nine-item-block feedback produced no detected transfer difference immediately or after one week. Small, specialized sample; this is not proof of equivalence everywhere. |
| Fade worked steps | Renkl et al. (2002), *From Example Study to Problem Solving: Smooth Transitions Help Learning*. [ERIC abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ658398). | Classroom/college experiments supported fading for near transfer; far transfer remained uncertain. Use support for unfamiliar procedures, then check independent work. |
| Mix related problem types | Rohrer, Dedrick & Burgess (2014), *The benefit of interleaved mathematics practice is not limited to superficially similar kinds of problems*. [Publisher abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-014-0588-3); [IES study review](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/study/80702). | A seventh-grade mathematics study favored interleaving on a delayed test. Domain-specific evidence; not a mandate to mix arbitrary subjects before basics are understood. |
| Check new applications | Butler (2010), *Repeated testing produces superior transfer of learning relative to repeated studying*. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20804289/). | Four adult laboratory experiments found benefits on one-week inferential questions. A reason to test transfer explicitly, not to assume real-world mastery. |
| Challenge transfer claims | van Eersel et al. (2016), *The Testing Effect and Far Transfer: The Role of Exposure to Key Information*. [Full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01977/full). | Undergraduate experiments suggest that focused exposure to key information accounts for part of a retrieval advantage. Transfer depends on comparison conditions and task design. |
| Respect preferences without assigning learning types | Pashler et al., *Learning Styles: Concepts and Evidence* (2008 volume; online 2009). [Publisher abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x). | The review found insufficient qualifying evidence for matching teaching to diagnosed learning styles. That does not invalidate access needs or learner preferences. |
| Offer useful access choices | CAST (2024), [Universal Design for Learning Guidelines 3.0](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/), guideline page. | A design framework for engagement, representation, and action/expression options. It is not a diagnosis or proof that every accommodation improves every learner's performance. |

## Product decisions, not research findings

- One question at a time, a short first lesson, an optional intake, and a concise resume cue are usability choices to reduce friction. Adapt them to the learner.
- Tomorrow → several days → roughly a week is a configurable starting review schedule. The appropriate intervals depend on the material, performance, time available, and retention goal.
- The observation states in the skill are a reporting convention, not a validated psychometric scale.
- An accessible lesson can still be inaccurate; an accurate lesson can still fail to teach. Verify sources and answers, then observe learner attempts separately.
- No cited study measured myelin changes caused by this skill. No evidence here establishes permanent memory, guaranteed grades, treatment effects, or Myelinate's own effectiveness.

## Learning-outcome evaluation

To evaluate a real pilot, agree on a small target and a baseline, record the assistance given, then use fresh but comparable delayed questions and a new application. Report the actual time interval, missing follow-ups, and limits. A single person's improvement is descriptive; a causal effectiveness claim needs a stronger comparison design. Consent to using the tutor is not consent to publishing learner data.
