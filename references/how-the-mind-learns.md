# How the mind learns, and what each mechanism asks of a tutor

Source review: **2026-10-10**. Most sources below were checked at abstract or snippet level; the few read in full are marked. Animal findings are labeled as animal findings. This file explains *why* the tutoring rules exist. It does not show that Myelinate changes anyone's brain.

## The one-paragraph model

A learner builds knowledge by **doing the retrieval, the prediction and the explanation themselves**. Those acts strengthen and reorganize what they already know. Activity that is repeated and spaced over days, then consolidated in sleep, makes the pathways durable. A tutor's job is to set up the right act at the right difficulty and to give honest feedback on the result. Then the tutor gets out of the way. Every time the tutor does the act for the learner, by stating the answer, completing the step, or agreeing with a guess, the learner skips the part that builds the pathway.

## Mechanisms

### 1. Practice that is used gets built: activity-dependent myelination (animal evidence)

- Neuronal activity drives the birth of oligodendrocyte precursor cells and new myelin, and blocking that response blocked the behavioral gain: Gibson et al. 2014, *Science* 344:1252304 ([full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4096908/); mice, optogenetic stimulation, not learning itself).
- New oligodendrocytes are needed to *learn* a new motor skill but not to perform an already learned one: McKenzie et al. 2014, *Science* 346:318–322 ([full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6324726/); mice, complex wheel). Early motor learning needs oligodendrocyte differentiation: Xiao et al. 2016, *Nat Neurosci* 19:1210 ([abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27455109/)).
- Disrupting new oligodendrocytes impaired spatial **memory consolidation** in adult mice (Steadman et al. 2020, *Neuron*, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31753579/)). New myelin was not needed to form a fear memory but was needed to **keep** it weeks later (Pan et al. 2020, *Nat Neurosci*, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32042175/)).
- Review of the mechanism: Fields 2015, *Nat Rev Neurosci* 16:756 ([abstract](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn4023)).
- Humans: six weeks of juggling changed a white-matter MRI signal (Scholz et al. 2009, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19820707/)). MRI measures such as fractional anisotropy are **not direct myelin measures** (Sampaio-Baptista & Johansen-Berg 2017, *Neuron*, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29268094/)).

**What a tutor takes from it:** durable skill comes from the learner's own repeated, effortful activity, spread over time, with consolidation between sessions. That supports the method's emphasis on learner-generated work and spaced return. It is a metaphor for practice and a motivation for spacing, not a measurement.

**Never claim:** that a session builds, measures, or speeds myelin; that MRI white-matter change equals myelin; that mouse results prove a tutoring method works.

### 2. Prediction error: being surprised by the answer is when the update happens

- Dopamine neurons signal the gap between expected and received outcome (Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997, *Science* 275:1593, [abstract](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593); monkeys, reward learning).
- In declarative memory, prediction error supported one-shot learning across five experiments (Greve et al. 2017, *J Mem Lang* 94:149, [summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5381756)).
- Errors made with high confidence are corrected *more* readily than low-confidence ones once feedback arrives: the hypercorrection effect (Butterfield & Metcalfe 2001, *JEP:LMC* 27:1491, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.27.6.1491)).
- Unsuccessful retrieval attempts followed by feedback improved later learning (Kornell, Hays & Bjork 2009, *JEP:LMC*, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19586265/)). Pretests, even failed ones, improved learning of the tested material (Richland, Kornell & Kao 2009, *JEP:Applied* 15:243, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19751074/)). Review: Metcalfe 2017, *Annu Rev Psychol* 68, "Learning from Errors" ([record](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-psych-010416-044022)).

**What a tutor takes from it:** get a **committed prediction or answer, and the learner's confidence, before you give any information**. A revealed answer with no prior commitment carries a much weaker error signal. An error is only useful if feedback follows it, so never leave a wrong answer standing.

### 3. Generation and retrieval: producing it beats reading it

- Generating an item yields better memory than reading it (generation effect; meta-analysis of 86 studies, Bertsch et al. 2007, *Mem Cogn* 35:201, [abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03193441)).
- Practice testing and spaced practice are the two highest-utility study techniques (Dunlosky et al. 2013, *PSPI*, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/)). Classroom quizzing improves learning (Yang et al. 2021, *Psych Bull* 147:399, 48,000+ students, [record](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2021-24563-001)).
- Retrieval produces brain activity that predicts later memory (Wing, Marsh & Cabeza 2013, *Neuropsychologia*, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23607935/); small fMRI sample). Mechanism review: van den Broek et al. 2016, *Trends Neurosci Educ* 5:52 ([abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2211949316300060)).
- Prompted self-explanation: g = 0.55 across 69 effect sizes (Bisra et al. 2018, *Educ Psychol Rev*; secondary summary). Original study: Chi et al. 1994, *Cognitive Science* 18:439 (24 eighth-graders).
- Engagement hierarchy, Interactive > Constructive > Active > Passive (Chi & Wylie 2014, *Educ Psychol*, [PDF](https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/chiwylie2014icap_2.pdf)). Reading your explanation is *active* at best. The learner explaining, predicting or arguing back is *constructive* or *interactive*.

**What a tutor takes from it:** every turn should end with the learner producing something. Your explanation is the cost; their production is the benefit.

### 4. Desirable difficulty, and its boundary

- Spacing, interleaving, testing and generation slow apparent progress and improve durable learning (Bjork & Bjork 2011, "Making things hard on yourself, but in a good way", [PDF](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf)).
- The boundary: a difficulty is desirable only if the learner can respond to it successfully, with the pieces in hand or reachable with a hint. Novices facing high-interaction material need worked examples first (cognitive load theory; expertise reversal, Kalyuga 2007, *Educ Psychol Rev* 19:509).
- Problem-solving *before* instruction beat instruction-first for conceptual knowledge and transfer across 53 studies, more so with well-designed productive failure, but not for young children (Sinha & Kapur 2021, *Rev Educ Res* 91:761, [doi](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105)).

**What a tutor takes from it:** aim for work the learner gets right most of the time with effort, roughly 70–85% (Rosenshine's ~80% guide). Below that, add support. Above it, fade support or raise the difficulty. Struggle with no path forward is not desirable difficulty, so give a foothold.

### 5. Working memory is small

- About four chunks when rehearsal is controlled (Cowan 2001, *Behav Brain Sci*, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11515286/)); the number varies with task and expertise.

**What a tutor takes from it:** one question per turn, short turns, and the worked example beside the problem rather than above a long scroll. In voice, spoken words disappear (transient-information effect), so keep turns to 1–3 sentences.

### 6. Attention drifts unless retrieval interrupts it

- Short tests placed inside an online lecture reduced mind-wandering and improved learning (Szpunar, Khan & Schacter 2013, *PNAS* 110:6313, [abstract](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1221764110)).

**What a tutor takes from it:** never run long exposition. Interleave a retrieval prompt every few exchanges.

### 7. Curiosity opens the gate

- Curiosity rises when people notice a gap in what they know (Loewenstein 1994, *Psych Bull* 116:75, [record](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/1994-41058-001)). High-curiosity trivia answers were remembered better, with reward-circuit activity (Kang et al. 2009, *Psych Sci* 20:963, [author PDF](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/WickCandleLearning.pdf)). Curiosity states also improved memory for incidental material, via hippocampus–dopamine circuits (Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath 2014, *Neuron* 84:486, [record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25284006/)).

**What a tutor takes from it:** open with a question or puzzle that exposes the gap, and hold the answer briefly so the gap stays open. That is a reason to ask first, not a license to withhold from someone who is stuck.

### 8. Consolidation happens offline, and sleep does real work

- Sleep supports consolidation of declarative and procedural memory (Diekelmann & Born 2010, *Nat Rev Neurosci*, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20046194/)). Sleep loss before *and* after learning impairs memory (Newbury et al. 2021 meta-analysis, [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8893218/)).
- New material that fits an existing schema consolidates much faster (Tse et al. 2007, *Science*, rats, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17412951/)).
- Retrieved memories can become modifiable again, which is called reconsolidation (Nader, Schafe & LeDoux 2000, *Nature* 406:722, rats, fear memory). That supports correcting a misconception right after it is retrieved. It does not prove that retrieval strengthens human memory through this route.

**What a tutor takes from it:** spaced sessions with sleep between them beat one long session. Protect sleep before an exam. Connect each new idea to something the learner already knows, by asking them to name the connection.

### 9. Stress and emotion

- Shields et al. 2017 (*Psych Bull* 143:636, 113 human studies; [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5436944/), read):
  - Stress just before or during **retrieval** impaired memory: g = −0.22 across 31 studies, consistent across conditions, and larger for emotional material.
  - Stress before **encoding** had no significant overall effect (g = −0.11). Moderator analysis showed it impaired memory unless it came just before study and was related to the material.
  - Stress shortly **after** encoding helped modestly (g = +0.21).

**What a tutor takes from it:** don't manufacture pressure to "make it stick". Stress at recall costs points, which is one more reason to rehearse in exam-like conditions *before* the exam, so the real one feels familiar (`print`, `study-plan`). Reduce threat, especially social threat around being wrong, so learners will commit to guesses. A learner afraid of being wrong hedges, and a hedge weakens the error signal.

### 10. Learners misjudge their own learning

- Massed study felt more effective than spacing even when spacing worked better (Kornell & Bjork 2008, *Psych Sci*, [abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02127.x)). Students in active-learning classes learned more but *felt* they learned less (Deslauriers et al. 2019, *PNAS* 116:19251, [article](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1821936116)).
- People overrate their grasp of how things work until asked to explain (Rozenblit & Keil 2002, *Cognitive Science* 26:521). Overconfidence ends study early and lowers retention (Dunlosky & Rawson 2012, *Learning and Instruction* 22). Review: Bjork, Dunlosky & Kornell 2013, *Annu Rev Psychol* 64:417 ([abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23020639/)).

**What a tutor takes from it:** "I get it" and fluent reading of your explanation are feelings, not evidence. Test it: ask them to explain the mechanism step by step, or to solve a fresh case. Tell learners plainly that effortful practice feels worse while it works better, so they don't abandon it.

## The AI-specific risk: the tutor that does the thinking

- High-school students with an answer-giving GPT-4 scored 48% higher during practice and 17% lower on the unassisted exam. A hint-only version avoided most of the harm (Bastani et al. 2025, *PNAS*).
- Language models drift toward agreeing with the user, flip correct answers under "are you sure?", and praise flawed work (Sharma et al. 2023, arXiv:2310.13548). A tutor that agrees with a wrong answer removes the prediction error the learner needed.

Every mechanism above has the same weak point: the learning happens in the learner's own act. An AI tutor that answers first, completes the step, or agrees too readily makes the session feel productive while removing the part that builds the pathway. See `skills/tutor/SKILL.md` §3–§3c for the operating rules.

## Claims this file does not support

- That Myelinate, or any tutoring session, measurably changes myelin or brain structure.
- That a particular schedule is research-optimal for every learner. Spacing intervals depend on the retention horizon (Cepeda et al. 2008).
- That struggle is always good. Unsupported struggle on material the learner can't reach is just failure.
- That reconsolidation or prediction-error neuroscience proves a specific classroom technique. The behavioral studies carry the practical weight. The neural work explains them and is mostly animal or small-sample imaging.
